# CMOS-inverter
A CMOS inverter is a fundamental building block of digital circuits, consisting of a PMOS and an NMOS transistor. It operates on the principle of complementary switching, where one transistor is ON while the other is OFF, ensuring low power dissipation. Working Principle of CMOS Inverter: When the input is logic ‘0’ (0V), the NMOS is OFF, and the PMOS is ON, pulling the output to logic ‘1’ (VDD). When the input is logic ‘1’ (VDD), the NMOS is ON, and the PMOS is OFF, pulling the output to logic ‘0’ (0V). This results in a sharp voltage transfer characteristic (VTC), making CMOS inverters ideal for digital switching. Propagation Delay (tp) Propagation delay is the time taken for the output to switch after a change in input. By adjusting the NMOS and PMOS widths, the inverter's speed and power consumption can be optimized. Layout Design Considerations The physical layout of a CMOS inverter affects its performance due to parasitic capacitances and resistances. Optimized layout techniques, such as poly-gate sharing, diffusion sharing, and minimal interconnect lengths, help reduce delay and improve circuit efficiency.
